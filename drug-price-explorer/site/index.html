    About the numbers
    • Part D spending is the gross drug cost on the claim: what Medicare, the plan and the beneficiary paid together, before any manufacturer rebate or other price concession. CMS is barred from publishing rebates, so these figures overstate Medicare's net cost, often by a wide margin for drugs with heavy rebates.
    • Part B spending is the full value of the product at the HCPCS-code level, including the Medicare payment and the beneficiary's share. Medicare pays 106% of the manufacturer's average sales price (ASP) for most separately paid Part B drugs.
    • List price is the wholesale acquisition cost (WAC) for a 30-day supply, as CMS published it in the negotiation fact sheets (2023 prices for the first cycle, 2024 for the second).
    • Negotiated price is the maximum fair price (MFP) for a 30-day supply. The binding MFP is set per package (NDC); the 30-day figure is CMS's summary.
    • The savings calculator scales a drug's gross spending by the ratio of negotiated price to list price and subtracts an assumed rebate. It ignores utilization changes, the 2025 Part D benefit redesign and the manufacturer discount program. Treat it as an illustration, not a forecast.
    • Outlier flag. CMS marks a year's per-dosage-unit average when reported dosage units make it unreliable. Flagged values are marked in the trend chart.
